\begin{abstract}
This report reconstructs the full methodological arc of the repository. The project began with score-based temporal graph (SBTG) statistics intended to reveal directed, lagged, and distributional neural relationships. Repeated improvements changed the architecture, score objective, derivative readout, regularization, scale, conditioning scheme, and inference wrapper. Those changes produced real but narrow advances: nonlinear edge-local SBTG improved over the original feature-bilinear window model; a residual-score temporal map made clean mean-channel recovery competitive with ridge VAR; and conditional variance improved held-out neural prediction. They did not produce a robust receptor-specific, anatomical, or physical-delay claim.

The decisive synthetic lesson was that a conditional-mean model is excellent when the truth is encoded in the mean, but mean-only summaries are mathematically blind when histories alter covariance, reliability, skewness, mixture occupancy, or higher-order path shape while leaving the mean fixed. Flexible conditional laws can predict those distributions better, yet derivatives of the learned law can remain noisy or wrong. Averaging conditional-mean effects over heterogeneous histories can also cancel signal and amplify finite-sample instability.

The current program therefore changes the scientific object. It defines a finite, supported, compatibility-aware contrast between complete model-generated histories, rather than an arbitrary coordinate edit. Direct weighting and sequential Monte Carlo estimate the corresponding future laws; progressive bridge SMC improves finite-particle approximation for selective queries. This is a model-relative predictive response, not an identified physical intervention. Support, cross-model disagreement, quiet-time controls, and learned-model error remain mandatory because a numerically healthy sampler cannot repair a wrong predictive model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Mathematical objects and why the original route became unstable}

\subsection{Conditional law, mean, and variability}

Let $H\in\mathcal H$ be a neural/stimulus history and $Y\in\R^d$ a future response. The fundamental predictive object is
\begin{equation}
 P_h(A)=\Pr(Y\in A\mid H=h), \qquad p_h(y)=p(y\mid h).
\end{equation}
The conditional mean and covariance are
\begin{equation}
 m(h)=\E[Y\mid H=h], \qquad \Sigma(h)=\E[(Y-m(h))(Y-m(h))^\top\mid H=h].
\end{equation}
A mean model estimates $m(h)$. It is optimal for squared-error prediction, and it recovers mean-driven synthetic wiring well. But if $P_{h_+}$ and $P_{h_-}$ have equal means and differ only in covariance, skew, mixture weights, reliability, or path topology, then $m(h_+)-m(h_-)=0$ even when the laws are different.

Dataset averaging creates a second difficulty. A reported average response
\begin{equation}
 \bar\Delta=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\{m(h_i^+)-m(h_i^-)\}
\end{equation}
can be noisy or nearly zero when local effects vary in sign across histories. More rows do not necessarily create more independent information when rows share worms, events, or overlapping time windows.

\subsection{History score, SBTG operator, and derivative amplification}

For a history direction $v$ and perturbation $T_{\epsilon,v}h$, define the infinitesimal history witness
\begin{equation}
 \ell_v(y;h)=D_v\log p_h(y)=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\log p_{T_{\epsilon,v}h}(y)\right|_{\epsilon=0}.
\end{equation}
It satisfies $\E_h[\ell_v(Y;h)]=0$ and, for a declared response feature $\phi$,
\begin{equation}
 D_v\E_h[\phi(Y)]=\E_h[\phi(Y)\ell_v(Y;h)].
\end{equation}
The conditional outcome score is $s_y(y;h)=\nabla_y\log p_h(y)$. The ideal SBTG mixed operator is
\begin{equation}
 K_v(y;h)=D_v s_y(y;h)=\nabla_y\ell_v(y;h).
\end{equation}
Thus SBTG sees a response-space gradient of the history witness. Recovering $\ell_v$ from $K_v$ requires integration, centering, support, and boundary conditions. A cross-moment of a learned joint-state score is not automatically a conditional-law derivative, a physical field, or a causal graph.

Hyv\"arinen score matching minimizes, up to constants,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{SM}}(\theta)=\E\!\left[\tfrac12\|s_\theta(Y,H)\|_2^2+\nabla_Y\cdot s_\theta(Y,H)\right].
\end{equation}
Denoising score matching instead regresses the score of a Gaussian-corrupted law. A small response-score error does not imply a small history derivative error:
\begin{equation}
 s_{\theta,n}\to s_y \quad\not\Rightarrow\quad D_v s_{\theta,n}\to D_vs_y.
\end{equation}
This was observed directly: autodifferentiation and finite differences agreed to relative error about $1.4\times10^{-4}$, while graph AUROC stayed near chance. The code was differentiating the learned model correctly; the learned derivative was the wrong scientific statistic.

\subsection{Finite supported contrast}

For $h_\pm=h\pm\delta v$, define
\begin{equation}
 M_\delta=\tfrac12(P_{h_+}+P_{h_-}),\qquad
 W_\delta(y)=\frac{1}{\delta}\tanh\!\left(\frac{\log p_{h_+}(y)-\log p_{h_-}(y)}{2}\right).
\end{equation}
The bounded witness $W_\delta$ represents the central signed contrast at a declared scale. More generally, if $\nu_c=\sum_r c_rP_{h_r}$, $\sum_rc_r=0$, and $M_c=C^{-1}\sum_r|c_r|P_{h_r}$ with $C=\sum_r|c_r|$, then
\begin{equation}
 W_c=\frac{d\nu_c}{dM_c},\qquad |W_c|\le C,\qquad
 \sum_rc_r\E_{h_r}[\phi(Y)]=\E_{M_c}[\phi(Y)W_c(Y)].
\end{equation}
This target can be estimated by normalized densities, density ratios, balanced classifiers, projected moments, or samples. It avoids differentiating a flexible model with respect to history, but it still requires overlap and a response feature capable of seeing the target change.

\subsection{Compatibility-aware repaired paths}

The later neural work uses complete generated path prefixes. For prediction cut $c$, source $j$, lag $\ell$, and source-window width $w$, let
\begin{equation}
 A_j(Z)=\frac1w\sum_{r=c-\ell-w+1}^{c-\ell}Z_{rj}.
\end{equation}
Training-fold quartiles $q_{j,.25},q_{j,.75}$ define soft source potentials
\begin{equation}
 G_j^a(Z)=\exp\!\left[-\frac12\left(\frac{A_j(Z)-q_{j,a}}{b_j}\right)^2\right],
 \quad b_j=\max(0.25\,\mathrm{IQR}_j,0.10).
\end{equation}
An anchor potential $G_{\mathrm{anc},j}(Z)=\exp[-\lambda C_j(Z,Y)]$ keeps non-source population context near the factual episode. The repaired path law is
\begin{equation}
 Q_j^a(dZ)\propto P_\theta(dZ\mid H_{c-w-\ell},U)G_{\mathrm{anc},j}(Z)G_j^a(Z).
\end{equation}
For future target feature $\phi_k$, the saved response per achieved source unit is
\begin{equation}
 M_{kj}^{(\ell)}=
 \frac{\E_{Q_j^{.75}}[\phi_k]-\E_{Q_j^{.25}}[\phi_k]}
 {\max(|\widehat A_{j,\mathrm{high}}-\widehat A_{j,\mathrm{low}}|,0.10)}.
\end{equation}
This resembles backtracking counterfactual reasoning because the past is revised while downstream dynamics remain generated by the same model. It is deliberately called a compatibility-aware, model-relative response rather than a structural counterfactual.

\subsection{Distributional-versus-mean circuit battery}

The comparable battery used $N=40$ latent nodes, a randomly observed $M=12$, $L=3$ lags, $T=20{,}000$ unit-stride steps, and sparse off-diagonal operators with Bernoulli density $0.05$. Per-lag spectral radii were $0.6/\sqrt L$ for mean operators, $1.0/\sqrt L$ for variance operators, and $3.0/\sqrt L$ for covariance routing. Each trajectory was clipped to $[-12,12]$; the observed $[H\mid Y]$ block was column-standardized. The primary metric was off-diagonal lag-union graph AUROC against the known observed-to-observed parent support. Held-out mean $R^2$ diagnosed whether the conditional mean carried usable signal.

\section{Conclusion}

The program did not discover that ``SBTG simply needs a larger neural network.'' It discovered a sequence of estimand distinctions. Joint-window score products could localize some mean-like dependence but were weak for the distributional mechanisms that motivated them. Conditional-score cleanup and derivative regularization ruled out several coding and numerical explanations. The strongest SBTG repair forced the score through a conditional-mean transition map, which made explicit that the recovered signal was primarily mean dynamics. Simple mean models consequently remained the strongest default.

Synthetic systems then showed exactly where this default ends. Covariance-only, skew-only, multimodal, reliability, and higher-order path changes can leave the conditional mean unchanged. Predictive densities and samplers can represent those changes, but downstream history derivatives can remain unstable, and dataset-level averaging can cancel heterogeneous effects. The viable alternative is not an unconstrained point derivative. It is a finite, supported, typed contrast whose response feature is declared before fitting and whose adapter passes an oracle gate.

Compatibility-aware repaired paths instantiate that idea for neural trajectories. A source history is softly constrained inside a complete generated population context, the constraint is released at a declared cut, and the subsequent learned dynamics generate the future law of interest. Progressive bridge SMC is currently the most accurate finite-particle estimator of this frozen learned-law target. The final scientific claim remains deliberately narrow: the result is a model-implied predictive response under compatible histories. It becomes useful only when predictive qualification, empirical support, cross-model agreement, particle diagnostics, quiet-time specificity, and prospective validation agree.
